1. What the computer model is

The model is a small image classifier trained on 21,397 public field photographs of cassava leaves from Uganda and tested on photographs from Ghana. It recognises five conditions: bacterial blight, brown streak disease, green mite damage, mosaic disease and healthy.

2. What "confidence" means

Confidence is adjusted on data the model has never trained on, so a confidence of 80% means the model is right about 80 times out of 100 on such leaves.

"A portion of my farm field or harvest is infected."

"How frequently the model is correct on similar leaf symptoms."

3. Why it sometimes says "Not sure"

When confidence is below a threshold set on that data, the app says 'not sure' instead of guessing, asks three short questions, and refers you to an extension officer.

shieldSafety Standard

If environmental glare, dust, or camera shake reduces certainty below the threshold, CassaCheck stops automated recommendations and connects you with an extension officer.

Field Best Practice

Best photo rules for accurate results

One single leaf Isolate one symptomatic leaf rather than the whole branch.
Fill the phone screen Hold the lens 15–20 cm away until the leaf spans edge-to-edge.
Bright morning daylight, no shadows Avoid harsh midday direct sun or casting your own body shadow over the leaf.
